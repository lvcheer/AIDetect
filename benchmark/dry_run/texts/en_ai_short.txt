The neighborhood library began preparing before the storm warning became urgent. Staff charged the emergency lamps, moved boxes away from the basement walls, and printed a short list of phone numbers in case the internet failed. The children’s librarian placed puzzles, paper, and pencils near the reading tables so families would have something quiet to do during an outage.

By late afternoon, volunteers had checked every window and filled two insulated containers with drinking water. They did not expect the library to become a shelter, but they knew that people might stop by for information or a place to charge a medical device. A handwritten sign explained which services could continue without electricity.

When the rain finally arrived, the building went dark for only twenty minutes. The preparations were barely needed, yet nobody considered the effort wasted. The staff had learned where the flashlights were, which doors were difficult to secure, and how quickly a familiar public room could become important when the rest of the neighborhood felt uncertain.
