# Formal Benchmark Data and Lawful-Source Plan

Plan version: `0.1-draft`

Status: planning only. This document does not authorise data download,
generation, annotation, or model execution. Every source and generator must be
reviewed again when the benchmark is frozen.

## 1. Purpose and decision rule

The formal corpus will test detector behaviour across Chinese and English,
writing domains, generators, text lengths, and editing conditions. It will not
be presented as proof of authorship or misconduct.

The independent unit for design and uncertainty is the **source lineage**, not
the number of text files. Segments, translations, continuations, edits, and
paraphrases derived from the same material are dependent observations and must
remain in one leakage component.

A candidate source is eligible only when all four questions can be answered
with written evidence:

1. Who or what created the text, and how is the reference label known?
2. Do the licence and access terms permit the planned collection, processing,
   reporting, and—if applicable—redistribution?
3. Can the exact document, version, retrieval date, and licence statement be
   recorded?
4. Can the entire lineage and its near duplicates be kept in one split?

`Unknown`, an absent licence, a repository code licence applied ambiguously to
data, or a low detector score are not acceptable evidence.

## 2. Core design

### Languages and domains

The core confirmatory corpus will contain `zh` and `en` in three matched
domains:

| Domain | Operational definition | Candidate lawful material |
|---|---|---|
| `general` | Expository or narrative prose for a general audience | individually verified public-domain works and Wikimedia texts |
| `academic` | Research abstracts or self-contained scholarly prose | PMC Open Access articles whose individual licence is approved; another source is required for Chinese before freeze |
| `professional` | Public-information, guidance, or administrative prose | pages explicitly offered under a reusable public-sector or open licence; a Chinese counterpart remains to be approved |

`application` writing is deferred from the confirmatory core. Genuine
applications create privacy and consent risks, while synthetic applications do
not provide a defensible human reference class. It may later be added as a
separate exploratory domain under an approved consent and de-identification
protocol.

If a lawful, provenance-matched source cannot be approved for both languages in
a domain, that domain will not be silently replaced in one language. It will
either be removed from the matched core or reported as an explicitly unpaired
exploratory set.

### Reference classes

- **Human:** documented human-authored text with no known generative-AI input.
  Historical public-domain work is permitted but cannot be the only human
  source, because famous texts may have appeared in model pretraining.
- **AI:** an output generated by a recorded model revision from a versioned
  prompt packet. The provider, model/revision, date, parameters, system prompt,
  user prompt, and post-processing must be retained.
- **Ambiguous or mixed:** excluded from binary primary metrics and placed in an
  exploratory set with a non-binary provenance description.

### Generators and held-out generator

At least three generator families are required, subject to access terms and a
separate provider review:

- two development generators may contribute to training, calibration, and the
  in-distribution test;
- one generator family and its pinned dated revision will be used only in
  `test/generator_held_out`;
- the held-out generator's outputs, labels, and detector results must not be
  inspected for model selection, calibration choice, threshold selection,
  preprocessing, or prompt-template revision.

Generator identities are not frozen in this plan. Before collection, an
eligibility table must record API or local-model terms, redistribution rights,
retention rules, immutable or dated version information, cost, and availability
for both languages. A provider name alone is not a reproducible revision.

Prompt topics, instructions, target lengths, and genres will be balanced across
generators. Exact prompts or prompt families used for held-out evaluation may
be paired across generators only when every paired output is placed in the same
test leakage component. No prompt or source packet used to develop a detector
or threshold may reappear in held-out testing.

## 3. Sample-size plan

Counting will use independent source lineages. Producing several edits or
segments from one lineage does not increase the effective sample count.

### Pilot, excluded from final testing

Collect `10–20` eligible lineages per planned language × domain × provenance or
generator cell. The pilot will estimate:

- eligibility, retrieval, generation, annotation, and detector failure rates;
- attainable length distributions and truncation;
- duplicate-cluster sizes and within-lineage dependence;
- variance of ROC-AUC and human false-positive rate at the candidate operating
  points.

Pilot records may be used for pipeline and rule development but will never be
moved into a final-test partition.

### Formal freeze rule

Final counts will be selected by a simulation based on pilot estimates and a
source-cluster bootstrap. The target is a 95% interval half-width of at most
`0.05` for language-level ROC-AUC and at most `0.03` for the primary
language-level human false-positive rate, when attainable. The simulation,
seed, assumptions, and chosen counts must be committed before final-test labels
or scores are inspected.

The following are reporting floors, not claims of statistical power:

- at least `200` independent human and `200` independent AI lineages per
  language in each primary test partition when pooled across domains;
- at least `50` independent human and `50` independent AI lineages in every
  language × domain subgroup reported confirmatorily;
- groups below the frozen floor are labelled `insufficient evidence`, even if a
  point estimate can be computed.

For the development-generator pool, source components will use a prespecified
`50% / 20% / 30%` train/calibration/in-distribution-test allocation unless the
pilot simulation justifies a documented revision. The held-out-generator test
is built separately and is never sampled into those proportions. Human
comparators assigned to the held-out partition must be separate lineages from
the in-distribution human test set.

## 4. Length and editing conditions

The pilot will define short, medium, and long bands from a language-appropriate
reference measure before formal collection. Character counts, word counts, and
each detector's token count will all be retained. Bands will not be redefined
after seeing detector performance. Detector-specific truncation is a measured
condition, not an exclusion criterion.

The confirmatory primary condition is `original`. Secondary robustness
conditions are:

- `human_light_edit`: spelling, punctuation, and local clarity edits under a
  written time-and-operation limit;
- `machine_paraphrase`: one pinned paraphraser and recorded settings;
- `human_deep_edit`: exploratory only until effort, permissible operations, and
  the mixed-authorship interpretation are defined.

Every derivative records `parent_document_id` and shares the original
`source_id`, near-duplicate component, and split. Editing conditions will be
balanced by design; failures and refusals remain in the audit log.

## 5. Source register and legal status

This register distinguishes the licence on data from the licence on repository
code. Approval applies at document or source-subset level, not merely at a
dataset name.

Gate A reviews use `source_eligibility_review_template.md`; the initial
Chinese-source findings are recorded in `gate_a_source_audit.md`.

| Candidate | Proposed role | Current status | Required action before use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project Gutenberg | English general/historical human material | conditional | verify each work's internal rights notice and UK/local copyright status; remove licence/trademark wrapper from evaluated prose; retain landing-page provenance |
| Chinese Wikisource | Chinese general/historical human material | conditional | verify the work-level public-domain or free-licence template and comply with page/contributor CC BY-SA attribution and share-alike terms |
| Wikimedia projects | multilingual general reference material | conditional/secondary | record page revision and licence attribution; audit mirrors and embedded third-party material; treat training contamination as a limitation |
| PMC Open Access Subset | English academic human material | conditional | use only an approved per-article licence (prefer `CC0` or `CC BY`); retrieve only through an authorised PMC service; never treat all PMC as reusable |
| *Language and Linguistics* Chinese issues | Chinese academic human material | conditional | use item-verified CC BY articles, prefer pre-generative-AI publication dates, and retain journal/issue attribution; report linguistics as one field rather than the whole academic domain |
| ChinaXiv | Chinese academic human material | conditional | filter at item/version level to Chinese CC BY or CC0 manuscripts; prefer deposits before `2022-11-30`; keep preprints distinct from peer-reviewed articles and exclude publisher PDFs |
| UK public-sector pages under OGL v3.0 | English professional human material | conditional | include only pages explicitly covered by OGL; store required attribution and check listed exemptions |
| HC3 | pipeline/development comparison only | excluded from primary test | use only source-specific subsets with verified rights; document that evaluated Hello-SimpleAI detectors may have trained on HC3 |
| RAID | optional English robustness comparison | pending | establish in writing whether its MIT repository licence covers distributed text and all upstream sources; it does not supply the Chinese core |
| M4 | optional multilingual robustness comparison | pending/excluded | obtain an explicit data licence and upstream-source audit; repository availability alone is insufficient |
| other web or Hugging Face datasets | none by default | unapproved | require an official data card, document-level provenance, licence compatibility, and contamination audit |

## 6. Contamination, leakage, and confounding controls

1. Search detector model cards and papers for named training datasets before a
   source is admitted. Known detector-training corpora cannot be a primary
   final test for that detector.
2. Record content hashes, normalised hashes, and near-duplicate clusters before
   splitting. Exact or near duplicates remain in one component.
3. Keep source packets and prompt families within one split. Topic matching
   across splits uses independently authored packets, not copied prompts.
4. Balance language, domain, target length, topic family, and collection period
   across provenance classes where feasible. Record residual imbalance rather
   than removing inconvenient results after scoring.
5. Separate historical public-domain, contemporary open-licence, and
   crowd-edited human sources in reporting. This prevents a source-style effect
   from being mistaken for a general human/AI distinction.
6. Preserve generation refusals, empty outputs, collection failures, and
   exclusions with reason codes. Replacing them after test inspection is not
   allowed.

## 7. Required provenance record

In addition to the existing manifest fields, the acquisition ledger must store:

- source title, creator when known, canonical URI, revision, retrieval date,
  rights statement URI, licence identifier, attribution text, and reviewer;
- whether raw text may be redistributed or only hashes/metadata/results may be
  released;
- source publication date and benchmark collection date;
- generator provider, family, immutable or dated revision, generation date,
  complete prompt packet, decoding parameters, and API/local software version;
- exclusion and transformation history.

Before manifest construction, the schema needs a reviewed revision adding at
least `prompt_family_id`, `rights_statement_uri`, `source_revision`,
`publication_date`, and a redistribution category. Until then, these fields
must not be hidden in free-text `notes` and treated as structured evidence.

## 8. Freeze gates and execution order

No data are collected until Gates A–C are approved. No formal model run occurs
until all gates are approved.

| Gate | Evidence required | Output |
|---|---|---|
| A — source legality | document-level rights template and attribution/redistribution decision | approved source register |
| B — generator legality | terms, versioning, retention, redistribution, cost, bilingual availability | eligible generator list |
| C — design | domain definitions, prompt families, length bands, editing rubric, pilot counts | pilot protocol |
| D — pilot review | failure rates, duplicate structure, variance/precision simulation | frozen counts and exclusions |
| E — schema and split | revised schema, leakage tests, deterministic seed and hashes | frozen manifest without test inspection |
| F — analysis freeze | detector list/revisions, score mappings, metrics, thresholds, bootstrap plan | signed benchmark version |
| G — execution | immutable inputs and approved environment | one-time formal results |

Any material change after Gate F creates a new benchmark version. Results from
the previous final test become development evidence and cannot remain an
unseen-test claim.

## 9. Decisions still requiring joint confirmation

The next planning decisions are deliberately left open:

1. whether the three-domain matched core is feasible after lawful Chinese
   academic and professional sources are identified;
2. the eligible generator shortlist and which family is held out;
3. whether raw texts will be released, access-controlled, or represented only
   by hashes and derived results;
4. the pilot budget and the precision/cost trade-off used to freeze final
   counts;
5. whether `human_light_edit` and `machine_paraphrase` enter the first formal
   release or a later robustness release.
